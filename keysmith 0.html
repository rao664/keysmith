Why we made this

Most password tools want you to sign up first. We always found that a bit silly. You just need a good password, so Keysmith gives you one and then gets out of your way.

No account, no email, no pop-ups. Open it, click, copy, done.

Three habits that matter most

  • One password per account. It's the single biggest win.
  • Make them long. Sixteen characters beats a short, clever one.
  • Turn on two-factor. Even a stolen password then won't be enough.

Not sure where to start?

Begin with your email. If someone gets into it, they can reset the password on almost everything else. Change that one first, then work through your bank and your phone account.

Don't try to fix everything in one evening. A few accounts a week is plenty.

    How it works

    Making a strong password with Keysmith takes four steps.

    1. Choose your settings. Pick a length and the types of characters or switch to Passphrase to use random words.
    2. Generate. Keysmith makes the password with your browser’s secure random generator. Nothing is sent to a server.
    3. Check the strength. The meter shows how strong it is in bits of entropy and the “Time to crack” button estimates how long an attacker would need.
    4. Copy and keep it. Copy it into your password manager. Your last 10 passwords stay in this browser and you can clear them any time.

    Reading the strength meter

    • Weak: under 50 bits. Easier for an attacker to guess.
    • Fair: 50 to 71 bits.
    • Strong: 72 to 99 bits.
    • Excellent: 100 bits or more.

    Frequently asked questions

    Quick answers about how Keysmith works.

    Is Keysmith free?

    Yes. There is no account no sign-up and no limit on how many passwords you make.

    Are my passwords sent to a server or saved?

    No. Passwords are made in your browser and are never sent anywhere. Your last 10 passwords are saved in your own browser on your device so they are still there after you reload. Use the “Clear saved passwords” button to delete them.

    How are the passwords generated?

    Keysmith uses your browser’s secure random number generator the same one used for cryptography. Each character is picked without bias and a password always includes at least one character from every type you turn on.

    How long should my password be?

    Use at least 12 characters and 16 or more for important accounts such as email and banking. Longer is stronger.

    What does “bits of entropy” mean?

    It measures how unpredictable a password is. Every extra bit doubles the number of guesses an attacker needs. Around 72 bits is strong and 100 bits or more is excellent.

    How is the “Time to crack” estimate calculated?

    It assumes an attacker who can make 10 billion guesses per second and who finds the password after trying half of the possibilities on average. Real attackers may be faster or slower. Sites that limit login attempts make guessing much slower.

    Is it safe to type my real password into the checker?

    The checker runs only in your browser and sends nothing. Even so the safest habit is to test a password that is similar to yours not the one you actually use.

    What is a passphrase?

    A passphrase is several random words joined together. It is easier to type and remember than random symbols. Add more words to make it stronger.

    Why should I use a password manager?

    It remembers a unique strong password for every account so you do not have to. Read more in our blog post.

    How to create a strong password

    A strong password is long unpredictable and used on only one account. Length matters most: every extra character multiplies the number of guesses an attacker needs.

    The rules that matter

    • Use at least 12 characters and 16 or more for important accounts.
    • Let a generator choose the characters instead of inventing them yourself.
    • Avoid names birthdays pet names and patterns such as qwerty or 123456.
    • Never use the same password on two accounts.

    Try it

    Make one with the generator on the generator page then check its “Time to crack” estimate.

    Passphrases vs. random passwords

    A passphrase is several random words joined together such as Cedar-Lantern-Orbit-Maple. It is easier to type and remember than a string of symbols and its strength comes from how many words you pick at random. Five or more is a good start.

    Which should you use?

    • Use a passphrase for things you must remember: your device login and your password manager.
    • Use a random character password for everything else and let a password manager remember it.

    The words must be chosen randomly. A song lyric a quote or a phrase that means something to you is much easier to guess.

    Why you should never reuse a password

    When a website is breached attackers take the leaked emails and passwords and try them on other sites. This is called credential stuffing. If you reuse a password one leak can open your email shopping and banking accounts.

    A strong password does not help if it is reused. The site that leaks it may have stored it poorly and you cannot control that.

    What to do

    • Give every account its own password.
    • Start with your email bank and phone accounts since they can reset everything else.
    • Change a password right away if a service tells you it was breached.

    Turn on two-factor authentication

    Two-factor authentication (2FA) asks for a second proof of identity in addition to your password. Even if someone learns your password they cannot sign in without it.

    Common types strongest first

    • Passkeys and hardware security keys.
    • Codes from an authenticator app.
    • Codes sent by text message. This is better than nothing but the weakest option.

    Where to start

    Turn it on for your email banking and social media accounts first. Save the backup codes somewhere safe and offline so you can still get in if you lose your phone.

    How to spot a phishing message

    Phishing messages try to trick you into handing over your password or a security code. They often pretend to be a bank a delivery company or your employer.

    Warning signs

    • Urgent threats such as “your account will be closed today.”
    • A sender address that does not match the company’s real domain.
    • Links you did not expect or a login page you reached from a message.
    • Any request for your password or a code. Real companies do not ask for these.

    If you clicked

    Do not enter anything. If you already typed a password change it immediately change it anywhere else you used it and turn on 2FA. To check an account type the website’s address yourself or open its app.

    Do you need a password manager?

    For most people yes. A password manager creates a unique password for each account stores them securely and fills them in for you. It also only fills a password on the matching website which helps protect you from fake login pages.

    How to use one well

    • Protect it with a long passphrase that you do not use anywhere else.
    • Turn on 2FA for the manager itself.
    • Choose one from a reputable provider. The one built into your phone or browser is a fine place to start.

    Avoid keeping passwords in a notes app a spreadsheet or on paper left in view.

    What to do after a data breach

    You get an email saying a company you use was hacked. It's a horrible feeling, but it's also very common, and you can sort it out in about twenty minutes.

    Do these in order

    1. Change the password for that account. Pick a brand new one, not a small tweak of the old one.
    2. Change it anywhere else you used it. This is why reusing passwords hurts so much.
    3. Turn on two-factor. If the site offers it, switch it on now.
    4. Watch your email for odd messages. Attackers often follow a breach with convincing fake emails.

    If the breach included card or bank details, call your bank. They've seen it many times and can help quickly.

    Why password rules are so annoying

    "Must contain a capital letter, a number and a symbol, but not your name." We've all stared at that box and sighed.

    Those rules were meant to stop easy guesses. The trouble is people end up with Summer2026!, which ticks every box and is still easy to guess.

    What actually works

    • Length matters more than odd characters.
    • Random beats clever, every time.
    • Let a generator or a password manager do the remembering.

    When a site has silly limits, such as no symbols or a maximum length, just switch those options off in the generator and make a new one.
